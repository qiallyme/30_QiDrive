# **CHAPTER 10 — The Hyperawareness Spiral**

When Every Detail Feels Like a Warning

Sometimes my mind becomes a surveillance system I never asked for.

I notice everything — every shift in tone, every micro-expression, every unfinished sentence, every pause that lasts half a second too long. Most people walk into a room and just… live. I walk in and immediately absorb:

The tension between two people across the room.

The way someone’s smile doesn’t reach their eyes.

The subtle irritation in a breath.

The disappointment tucked into a shrug.

The avoidance in someone’s posture.

It all hits me at once like atmospheric pressure dropping before a storm.

Hyperawareness is not paranoia.

It is pattern recognition sharpened by years of needing to detect danger before it detonated.

My nervous system learned early:

If you see it coming, maybe you can stop it.

If you understand the mood, maybe you won’t get blamed.

If you anticipate the explosion, maybe you can shield yourself in time.

The irony is that hyperawareness does not protect me now.

It exhausts me.

When I’m spiraling, I don’t just see the signs — I interpret them, magnify them, pre-live their consequences. One shift in tone becomes “They’re upset.” Then “It’s my fault.” Then “This will blow up.” Then “I should’ve prevented this.” Then “I am the problem.”

All of that happens in seconds.

A chain reaction born from the ghosts of rooms I grew up in.

If I could turn it off, I might finally know what calm feels like.

But the alarms have no off switch.

Only volume levels.

Hyperawareness is not a flaw.

It’s a relic of survival.

But relics get heavy when you’re trying to live a life instead of escape one.
