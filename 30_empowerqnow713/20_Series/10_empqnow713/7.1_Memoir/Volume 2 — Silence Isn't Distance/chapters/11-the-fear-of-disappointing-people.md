# **CHAPTER 11 — The Fear of Disappointing People**

When Expectations Feel Like Landmines

I wish people understood how terrified I am of letting them down.

Not because they’re demanding.

But because my mind lives in a never-ending simulation of every possible disappointment I might cause.

Someone texts me.

I take too long to answer.

I imagine their face tightening.

Their tone changing.

Their trust fading.

Someone needs help.

I’m already overloaded.

I imagine them thinking I’m selfish.

Unreliable.

Cold.

Someone asks, “Are you okay?”

I don’t know how to explain the storm inside me without sounding dramatic, so I shrug it off.

Then I imagine them feeling shut out.

Rejected.

Unimportant.

Every moment becomes a tightrope walk between honesty and obligation, between capacity and guilt.

Not disappointing people feels like a moral responsibility.

As if my worth depends on being dependable, agreeable, useful, emotionally accessible, never burdensome.

It comes from childhood — where disappointing someone felt dangerous.

Where saying no meant chaos.

Where making a mistake meant punishment.

Where needing something meant being told you were too sensitive.

So now, as an adult, the fear of disappointing people isn’t just discomfort.

It’s dread.

A sinking feeling in my stomach.

A tightening in my throat.

A voice whispering, “You’ll lose them if you fail them.”

And every small misstep confirms the prophecy.

Every late reply feels like betrayal.

Every forgotten task feels like abandonment.

Every boundary feels like cruelty.

But I’m learning — slowly — that my value is not conditional.

That I can love people without sacrificing myself.

That disappointment is a part of being human, not a sign of being unworthy.

But it still sits with me like a shadow.

Quiet.

Heavy.

Impossible to fully escape.
