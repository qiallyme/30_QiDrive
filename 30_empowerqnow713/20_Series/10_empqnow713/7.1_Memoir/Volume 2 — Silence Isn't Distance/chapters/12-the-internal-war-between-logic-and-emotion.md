# **CHAPTER 12 — The Internal War Between Logic and Emotion**

When Your Mind and Body Don’t Agree

There is a specific kind of suffering reserved for people who feel deeply but think analytically.

A war between two systems that both believe they’re saving your life.

Logic says:

“It’s not that serious.”

“They didn’t mean it.”

“You’re overthinking this.”

“You’re safe.”

Emotion says:

“Something is wrong.”

“They’re upset.”

“You failed.”

“You’re losing them.”

“Fix it now.”

Both voices scream from different corners of my mind, and I become the battlefield they’re fighting on.

Emotion feels everything in real time.

Logic watches from the balcony, exhausted.

Emotion says, “This hurts.”

Logic says, “But this isn’t rational.”

Emotion says, “I’m scared.”

Logic says, “You have no evidence.”

Emotion shouts.

Logic lectures.

And I’m stuck between them, unable to move without betraying one side.

The worst part is that both voices are smart.

Both have receipts.

Both make sense.

And neither knows how to make peace with the other.

When the war escalates, the body gets involved:

My chest tightens.

My vision blurs.

My stomach flips.

My breathing quickens.

Emotion hijacks the steering wheel.

Logic bangs on the window.

And when the system overloads, silence becomes the only exit.

Not because I’m calm.

But because I’m too overwhelmed to choose a side.

Eventually the storm settles, and I’m left sorting through the debris — the misunderstandings, the apologies, the exhaustion, the spirals.

I wish the world understood that I’m not dramatic.

I’m not reactive.

I’m not unstable.

I’m just someone with a body that feels like a siren

and a mind that feels like a philosopher

and a soul stuck trying to keep them from killing each other.
