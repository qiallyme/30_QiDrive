# **CHAPTER 13 — The Mask Slips**

When You Can’t Pretend Anymore

There comes a point where the performance cracks.

Where the smile falters.

Where the polite nod doesn’t hide the trembling underneath.

Where the carefully-regulated tone gives way to a whisper of truth.

Masking works — until it doesn’t.

I can appear calm, collected, funny, patient, put-together, stable, supportive.

I can regulate myself so others don’t have to.

I can carry the emotional load of a room without letting anyone see the weight.

But when the mask slips, even for a second, everything I’ve been holding back rushes to the surface like a breached dam.

People look at me differently.

Some with concern.

Some with confusion.

Some with disappointment, because they thought I was stronger than this.

They don’t realize the mask wasn’t strength.

It was survival.

The truth is:

I’m tired.

Not normal tired — soul tired.

Nervous-system tired.

“I’ve been holding too much for too long” tired.

The moment the mask slips, all the suppressed panic, sadness, overwhelm, and exhaustion rise at once.

It feels like drowning in air.

And I hate being seen like that.

Not because vulnerability is shameful —

but because it feels dangerous to let people see the weight I’ve carried silently for so long.

When the mask cracks, I don’t break.

I finally breathe.

But breathing in front of others still feels like a risk I don’t know how to take.
