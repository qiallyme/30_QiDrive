Short answer: a **1974 Washington quarter** in everyday pocket change is usually worth **face value (25¢) to about $1–$3**, *unless* it’s in exceptional condition or has a rare error/variety. citeturn0search1turn0search2

**Standard value ranges (US coins):**  
- **Circulated (normal wear)** — ~**$0.25–$1** (common). citeturn0search1turn0search9  
- **Uncirculated (mint state)** — often **$2–$8+**, depending on grade. citeturn0search1turn0search11  
- **High-grade (MS65 and above)** — $10–$30+ (many listings cluster here). citeturn0search1turn0search10  
- **Exceptionally high grades (MS67+) or certified by major graders** — can be **much higher** in rare cases (some auction examples exceed $2,000 for superb certified coins). citeturn0search14

**Rare or error coins:**  
- Some **minting errors** (off-center strikes, doubled dies, missing/extra letters, unusual planchet issues) can command **$50 to several hundred, or more**, depending on rarity and collector demand. citeturn0search1turn0search4  
- Truly exceptional or highly documented error pieces occasionally fetch thousands at auction, but that’s well outside typical results. citeturn0search14

**Proof coins:**  
- **1974-S Proof quarters** (from proof sets) are usually worth a few dollars in typical condition, more if deeply mirrored or graded high. citeturn0search1

**Key takeaway:** Unless your particular quarter is **uncirculated and graded very high** or has a **recognizable error/variety**, its worth is basically its face value to a few dollars to a collector.
