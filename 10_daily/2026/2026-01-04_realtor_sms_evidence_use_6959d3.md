## Bottom line
**This conversation helps your case if handled correctly. It does not hurt it.**  
It is **context evidence**, not liability evidence. It supports **lack of notice, confusion created by the process, and post-judgment chaos**, but it must be **framed tightly** and **not over-relied on**.

The risk is not the content itself — the risk is *how* you present it.

---

## Why this helps (when used correctly)

### 1. Confirms **no actual notice**
The realtor repeatedly says:
- “You can find it on MyCase”
- “Looks like they sent something in May”
- Asks **you** whether you received statements or dates

That supports:
- You were **not actually informed**
- Even professionals had to **infer from public records**
- No one could point to **direct service or confirmed notice**

This aligns with your core claim: **lack of constitutionally sufficient notice**.

---

### 2. Shows **reasonable diligence on your part**
You:
- Asked questions
- Tried to understand timelines
- Explored mitigation (short sale, communication with lender)
- Did not ignore the situation

That undercuts any argument that you “sat on your rights.”

---

### 3. Demonstrates **systemic breakdown**
The conversation shows:
- Lender stopped communicating
- Realtor could not get clear answers
- Sheriff sale already happened without your knowledge
- Everyone is relying on *MyCase after the fact*

That supports a **due process failure narrative**, not a personal failure.

---

## What does NOT help (and how to neutralize it)

### ⚠️ Oversharing vulnerability
Messages about:
- Sleeping in your car
- Asking for $3
- Emotional distress

These are **humanly understandable**, but legally:
- They are **not necessary**
- They risk being misread as instability (even unfairly)

**Solution:**  
You do **not** submit the full thread raw. You submit a **curated excerpt** with a declaration explaining relevance.

---

### ⚠️ Realtor opinions ≠ legal authority
Statements like:
- “They usually come and change locks”
- “Maybe because of holidays”

These are **not evidence of legality**.

**Solution:**  
Use them only to show **confusion and inconsistency**, not as proof of lawful process.

---

## How to document this properly (important)

### Step 1: Preserve the originals
- Keep the screenshots exactly as-is
- Export the thread from iMessage if possible (PDF or full-res images)
- Do **not** annotate the originals

---

### Step 2: Create a **declaration**, not just an exhibit
You do **not** dump texts into the record alone.

You add a declaration like:

> “Attached as Exhibit __ are excerpts of a text conversation with a licensed real estate professional in December 2025. These messages are offered **not for the truth of legal conclusions**, but to demonstrate Defendant’s lack of notice, efforts to understand the status of the property, and the absence of clear communication from Plaintiff or the Court prior to removal.”

This avoids hearsay issues.

---

### Step 3: Curate the excerpts
Include only messages that show:
- Confusion about dates
- Reliance on MyCase
- No confirmation of notice
- Post-sale discovery

Exclude:
- Requests for money
- Emotional venting
- Irrelevant background

You are allowed to submit **partial excerpts**.
