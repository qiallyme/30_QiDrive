### 🚗 Driver vs Passenger — different legal positions

In entity["state","Indiana","US state"]:

- **You (driver)** are the one they stopped → they can require:
  - License, registration, insurance
  - Basic compliance with the stop

- **Passenger** is **not automatically detained**
  - They **do not have to answer questions**
  - They **can refuse to speak**
  - They generally **don’t have to provide ID** unless there’s a specific reason

---

### 👮 Can police talk to your passenger?

Yes — but there’s a catch:

- Police can **ask** your passenger questions  
- But your passenger has the right to say:
  - “I don’t want to answer questions”
  - “Am I being detained?”

If the officer **does not have reasonable suspicion** that the passenger did something wrong, they **can’t force them to engage**

---

### 🚨 When it changes (important)

The situation shifts if:

- Officer claims **reasonable suspicion** (something looks illegal)
- There’s a **probable cause situation** (drugs, weapons, warrants, etc.)
- It becomes a **safety issue** (they can order occupants out of the car — that part *is* allowed under cases like *Pennsylvania v. Mimms*)

At that point:
- Passenger may be **detained temporarily**
- ID might be required **if they’re being lawfully detained**

---

### 💰 Rideshare specifically (Lyft/Uber / cash rides)

Legally:
- Giving someone a ride (even for money) is **not automatically illegal**
- BUT:
  - If you’re doing it off-app (cash rides), it *can* raise questions
  - It’s usually a **regulatory issue**, not a criminal one

Police might ask:
- “Do you know this person?”
- “Are you being paid?”

You are **not required to volunteer extra info** beyond what’s necessary for the stop
