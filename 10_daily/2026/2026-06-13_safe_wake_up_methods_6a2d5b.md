---

### **Assistant**
The **closest safe household substitute is: nothing chemical.** Don’t make DIY smelling salts.

Use this instead:

1. **Say their name loudly + tap their shoulders firmly.** Red Cross calls this “shout-tap-shout” and says to check responsiveness for no more than 10 seconds. citeturn285335search0  
2. **Check breathing.** Look for chest rise, listen/feel for breath.  
3. **If they’re unresponsive, breathing weird, gasping, or you can’t wake them: call 911 now.**  
4. **If they are breathing but won’t wake:** put them on their side/recovery position and keep watching breathing. citeturn285335search5  
5. **If opioids might be involved** — morphine, oxy, fentanyl, heroin, etc. — give **Narcan/naloxone** if available and still call 911. CDC says naloxone can reverse opioid overdose. citeturn285335search7  

**Do not use:** ammonia, bleach, glass cleaner, vinegar mixtures, rubbing alcohol, perfume blasted in their face, or anything strong under the nose. Ammonia inhalants can irritate/constrict airways, and prolonged exposure can cause serious lung harm. citeturn285335search13turn285335search4  

Best “wake-up” household move: **light on, loud voice, shoulder tap, cool damp cloth on forehead/neck, fresh air** — but if they’re truly hard to wake, **that’s 911 territory.**

Do this:

1. **Lights on.**

3. **Sit him upright**, don’t just poke him while he’s curled up.
4. **Cold damp cloth** on face/neck.

Do **not** shove chemicals, cleaners, alcohol, ammonia, perfume, etc. under his nose. We’re waking a man, not exorcising a Victorian widow.

**Safety check though:** if he cannot answer simple questions, is breathing weird/gasping, has blue/gray lips, chest pain, severe confusion, or you truly can’t wake him with loud voice + shoulder tap within about 10 seconds, call **911**. Red Cross guidance is to check responsiveness with shout-tap-shout and escalate if they do not respond or are not fully awake. citeturn478469search0turn478469search4
